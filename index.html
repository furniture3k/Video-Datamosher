01How a mosh works

Video files mostly store movement.

A full picture, a keyframe, now and then. In between, only which blocks moved where. Take away the keyframe at a cut and the next shot's movement gets played on the last picture.

03The editor

And around it, a whole editor.

A multi-track timeline with the tools you'd expect from a desktop editor, so you can cut the video you want to break in the same place you break it.

  1. Media Import video, photos, GIFs and music, record your camera or screen, or generate footage to try things on (city lights, a sunset, a night drive).
  2. Player Plays your cut straight away from the source files, and the rendered mosh as soon as it's ready.
  3. Presets, Randomize, Variations presets, a chaos dial and a grid of variations to breed from. Every result has a seed, so you can get it back.
  4. Inspector Every setting comes with a line explaining what it does.
  5. Mosh FX lanes Effects are bars on the timeline: drag, resize and stack them, or let them fire on the beat.
  6. Transitions Melt, Bloom melt, Glitch cut and more on any cut, next to dissolves, wipes, circle and box crops, and a whip that goes the way the picture moves.
  7. Export MP4, WebM, GIF, PNG frames or the raw broken AVI, up to 4K and 60 fps, with sizes made for Reels, TikTok and Shorts.
Timeline
Video and audio tracks, as many as you like. Magnetic main track, ripple trims, blade, slip and roll.
Clips
Speed 0.05–16×, speed ramps, reverse, freeze frames, keyframed position, zoom, rotation and opacity, with motion blur on the moves.
Colour
Colours kept true from your clip to the file, HDR phone footage brought to normal range, .cube LUTs, chroma key, adjustment layers and scopes.
Music
Zoom punches, shakes and speed ramps on the beat, effects that fire on every beat or bar or swell with the bass, and an edit cut to the song for you.
Tracking
Follow an object, a face or a flat surface through the shot and pin a title, a shape or a clip to it. One-click stabilize.
Cut-outs
Tap a subject and it's followed through the clip, so titles, shapes and other clips can sit behind it. Models run on your device.
Type and shapes
Gradient and per-word type, callouts, leader lines, tape, paper and stickers, with shadows and outlines on anything.
Collage
Polaroid cards, animated masks, pop-ins on the beat, split screens that swap and fake phone screens that zoom.
Steal this edit
Pick a video you like and your clips are cut the same way: its rhythm, frame rate, colours and frame.
Captions
Whisper, running on your device. Clean, karaoke, or melting with the mosh. Export .srt or .vtt.
Live
Play effects from MIDI knobs and pads, or over OSC in the desktop app, and record the take.
Output
Up to 4K (2160×3840 for vertical video) and 60 fps. 10 minutes in the browser, 30 in the desktop app. Vertical crops that follow the action, seamless loops.

06Questions

Questions.

More in the docs, or ask on GitHub.

What is datamoshing?

Video files mostly store changes, not pictures: a full picture now and then (a keyframe) and in between only how blocks of it move. Remove a keyframe and the movement of the next shot gets applied to the old picture, so it melts. Repeat a movement and the picture smears and blooms. Read more.

Is it really free? Are my videos uploaded?

Yes, and no. moshpit is free software (GPL-3.0). Your videos never leave your device: decoding, moshing and encoding all happen in your browser or in the desktop app.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. Phones get their own editor with a big preview, a swipe-to-scrub timeline and tools that follow what you select. Add it to your home screen to use it like an app.

Can I make Reels, TikToks and Shorts with it?

Yes: that is what most people make. Start from a template, or set the project to 9:16. Made for in Export sets the size (1080×1920, or 2160×3840 for 4K) and shows the apps’ safe zones in the player, zoom punches and shakes land on the beat of your song, and colours and HDR phone footage come out the way they looked.

How long and how big can videos be?

In the browser, up to 10 minutes and comfortably 1080p; 1440p and 4K work for short videos. The desktop app goes to 30 minutes and is much faster at 4K.

Why does the exported AVI look different in every player?

Because a moshed AVI is broken on purpose and every player hides the damage in its own way. MP4, WebM and GIF exports contain exactly what the preview shows.

Can I help?

Please do. Report bugs, suggest features or send code on GitHub, improve a translation, and share what you make in the gallery.
